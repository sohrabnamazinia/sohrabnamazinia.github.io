<!-- ========== DASHSys @ VLDB 2026 ========== -->
<div class="talk-card">
  <div class="talk-card-header">
    <div class="talk-meta">
      <div class="pub-status pub-status--accepted" style="margin-bottom:12px;">Invited Talk</div>
      <h2 class="talk-title">ASMR: Agentic Schema Generation for Ship Maintenance Report Writing</h2>
      <div class="talk-event"><i class="fas fa-map-marker-alt"></i> DASHSys 2026 @ VLDB — Workshop on Systems for Data-centric Agents with Human-in-the-loop &nbsp;·&nbsp; Boston, MA &nbsp;·&nbsp; September 2026</div>
      <p class="talk-desc">
        Presented ASMR, a modular two-agent framework that discovers compact, reusable schemas from historical ship maintenance narratives. The talk walked through the Field Generation Agent, which extracts semantic concepts via adaptive multi-granularity clustering, and the Structural Optimizer Agent, which uses reinforcement learning to trim and merge candidates into a compact, non-redundant schema — nearly doubling informativeness while shrinking schema size by almost 8×.
      </p>

<!-- ========== VLDB 2026 CONFERENCE ========== -->
<div class="talk-card">
  <div class="talk-card-header">
    <div class="talk-meta">
      <div class="pub-status pub-status--published" style="margin-bottom:12px;">Poster Presentation</div>
      <h2 class="talk-title">Lower-Bound Distance Queries under Partial Information</h2>
      <div class="talk-event"><i class="fas fa-map-marker-alt"></i> International Conference on Very Large Data Bases (VLDB 2026) &nbsp;·&nbsp; Boston, MA &nbsp;·&nbsp; September 2026</div>
      <p class="talk-desc">
        Co-presented our VLDB 2026 work on answering lower-bound distance queries in a metric-space graph where only a subset of edge distances is known — without relying on any black-box distance oracle. Discussions at the poster covered the query-based model and how the framework trades off preprocessing overhead, query time, and bound tightness with provable guarantees under metric assumptions.
      </p>
